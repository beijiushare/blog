# In Wuhan

Translated by DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. The Chinese original prevails.

> "High rises the Yellow Crane Tower, the jade flute grieves;
> The river's sound winds long around the emerald window."

It is already my fourth year in Wuhan; most of the months around my twentieth year have been spent here.

Wuhan is usually noisy and bustling: people grabbing breakfast finish their hot dry noodles in the shops across the road, pull out a tissue to wipe their mouths, and slip through the gaps in the traffic and the seams in the crowd; couples are forever clinging to each other by the roadside, pulling and hugging in farewell; sedans swish past right before your eyes along the pavement…

> The Yangtze is very calm—a turbulent calm that hides menace.

Old men in nothing but a pair of baggy shorts leap from the bank one after another like dumplings dropped into a pot, quite dashingly, drawing cheers all around. As for getting me up close to those seemingly calm waves—I would rather not. But the Yangtze at night makes one eager to draw near. When the wind is not too strong, around eight o'clock, walking on the riverside path, adults and children crowd inside the halos of the street lamps, flailing at badminton; the sunset power-walkers clap in rhythm and stride past with shouts. When you come to the stone steps, you can walk all the way down and sit on the sloping bank; the waves lap with a "lang lang" sound, the Yangtze River Bridge seizes your view, its bright lights stretching all the way to the far shore and spilling from the towering buildings onto the river; the shimmering ripples sway, and now and then seem to catch something—but look closely, and there is nothing at all…

It really is such a delight!
